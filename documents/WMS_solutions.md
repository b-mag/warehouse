# Forge WMS - Common Warehouse Problems and How We Solve Them

**Scope:** These are common, real-world problems people experience operating a Warehouse Management System (WMS). This document maps each problem to the solution Forge has implemented (or attempted to mitigate), with one or two source files that address it. Deferred problems are listed separately under Future Work.

**How to read this:** Forge is a three-way split - a reusable, simulation-agnostic `WMS_Core` (Domain + Application), a pluggable `Input_Driver` (the Phase 1 Simulation), and a pure `Game` visualization layer. Everything below lives in the `WMS_Core` unless noted, which is what makes it reusable as a real warehouse digital-twin foundation.

**Status at time of writing:** WMS_Core (Domain + Application) is complete and verified - clean build, 153 passing tests, including property-based determinism tests. Infrastructure, Api, Simulation driver, and Game layers are in progress.

## Part 1 - Common WMS Problems We Have Solved

| # | Common WMS Problem | Forge Solution | Source File(s) |
|---|--------------------|----------------|----------------|
| 1 | **Shipping expired or soon-to-expire stock / spoilage waste.** Perishable inventory is shipped in the wrong order, so product expires on the shelf instead of being consumed. | **First-Expired-First-Out (FEFO) selection.** Lots are selected strictly in ascending expiry order, with fully deterministic tie-breaking (FEFO priority, then lot id). Expired lots are excluded from selection. Partial fulfillment reports a shortfall rather than silently under-shipping. | `src/Forge.Domain/Fulfillment/FefoSelector.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Fulfillment/FulfillmentResult.cs` |
| 2 | **Stale shelf-life data.** Systems track a static "expiry date" but do not model shelf-life decaying continuously as time passes, so risk is discovered too late. | **Expiry decay over simulated time.** Remaining shelf-life is a deterministic function of expiry timestamp and current clock time. Advancing the clock reduces shelf-life; crossing zero raises exactly one expiry event; already-expired lots are never decayed twice. | `src/Forge.Domain/Gels/GelLot.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Gels/Formulation.cs` |
| 3 | **Cold-chain breaks going unnoticed.** Temperature excursions are not detected or attributed to specific product, so at-risk stock keeps moving. | **Temperature zones with excursion detection.** Each reading is appended to a lot's history in timestamp order; a reading outside the assigned zone range raises an excursion event and flags the lot at-risk. Detection is deterministic for identical inputs. | `src/Forge.Domain/ColdChain/TemperatureZone.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/ColdChain/TemperatureReading.cs` |
| 4 | **Overfilling locations and vehicles.** Put-aways exceed rack/zone capacity, or loads exceed vehicle cargo limits, causing rework and safety issues. | **Hard capacity constraints.** Put-away into a zone and loading onto a starship are permitted only when stored/loaded + requested <= capacity; violations are rejected with an error that reports both the requested amount and the remaining capacity. Negative/zero quantities and negative capacities are rejected. | `src/Forge.Domain/Capacity/CapacityRule.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/ColdChain/TemperatureZone.cs` |
| 5 | **Poor slotting (random put-away).** Fast-movers end up in hard-to-reach locations, inflating travel time and picking cost. | **Velocity/affinity-based slotting.** Put-away chooses a temperature-compatible zone with available capacity, preferring placements that minimize expected future travel for higher-velocity gels; ties broken deterministically by zone id. A naive first-available strategy is included for A/B comparison. | `src/Forge.Application/Slotting/VelocityAffinitySlottingStrategy.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Slotting/NaiveFirstAvailableStrategy.cs` |
| 6 | **Unmanaged labor cost and worker utilization.** Labor is the largest controllable warehouse cost, yet many systems cannot attribute cost to tasks or measure utilization. | **Explicit labor-cost modeling.** Each task carries an estimated duration; completed tasks accrue cost = (duration + travel time) x worker hourly rate. Workers are assigned only while on shift; total labor cost and per-worker utilization are exposed. Accrual is deterministic for identical inputs. | `src/Forge.Application/Labor/LaborCostCalculator.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Labor/LaborLedger.cs` |
| 7 | **Dock congestion.** Inbound receiving and outbound loading compete for the same dock doors with no scheduling, creating trailer/vehicle contention. | **Dock bay scheduling as a constrained shared resource.** Dock bays hold a schedule of inbound/outbound time slots; competing assignments for an occupied slot are rejected and queued; utilization and contention backlog are exposed; freed slots are assigned to the earliest queued operation deterministically. | `src/Forge.Application/Docks/DockScheduler.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Docks/DockSchedule.cs` |
| 8 | **The warehouse-as-bottleneck is invisible.** Managers cannot see whether the constraint is receiving, storage, labor, or shipping. | **Observable receiving/outbound backlogs and throughput.** The warehouse is modeled as a middle bottleneck between continuous inbound and outbound flow; receiving and outbound backlog sizes and inbound/outbound throughput rates are exposed as live metrics. | `src/Forge.Application/Simulation/WarehouseMetrics.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Simulation/ApplyTickRulesHandler.cs` |
| 9 | **Delivery/shipping windows missed.** Loads happen outside the vehicle's scheduled window or exceed cargo capacity. | **Starship loading windows and capacity.** Loads are permitted only inside a scheduled window; out-of-window loads are rejected and report the next window start; picks for loading follow FEFO; a closing window raises an event reporting loaded quantity and any shortfall. | `src/Forge.Domain/Vessels/Starship.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Loading/StarshipLoadingService.cs` |
| 10 | **Physical travel modeled as a hand-waved distance.** Travel time is guessed, so labor and throughput estimates are wrong. | **Spatial grid with agent pathfinding.** Agents occupy real grid cells and move along an A* planned path; task travel time is derived from the traversal time of that path. Path computation is deterministic for identical grid + endpoints. | `src/Forge.Domain/Spatial/AStarPathPlanner.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Spatial/WarehouseGrid.cs` |
| 11 | **Agents colliding / double-booked resources.** Two workers try to use the same aisle segment, dock, or pick face at once - unrealistic and non-reproducible. | **Path reservations and single-occupancy resources.** An agent reserves each path segment for the interval it occupies it; overlapping reservations are prevented (mutual-exclusion), forcing re-plan or hold. Dock bays and pick faces are single-occupancy. Contention resolves deterministically (tie-break by agent id). | `src/Forge.Domain/Spatial/ReservationLedger.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Docks/SingleOccupancyRegistry.cs` |
| 12 | **Demand that does not evolve.** Static demand assumptions ignore seasonality and trend shifts, so planning drifts out of date. | **Evolving colony demand profiles.** Colonies consume via demand profiles that shift at trend boundaries; demand generation is deterministic and reproducible for identical profile + time + seed. Profile modeling lives in the Domain; authoritative generation is in the Simulation driver, not the Game. | `src/Forge.Domain/Colonies/DemandProfile.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Colonies/TrendBoundary.cs` |
| 13 | **No forecasting, or forecasts you cannot trust/adjust.** Automated forecasts are either absent or applied blindly with no human control or audit trail. | **ML predictor interface + human-in-the-loop accept/override.** A predictor interface with a statistical baseline produces forecasts; an operator can accept, or override within a validated range, or let a configurable deadline (1-168h, default 24h) auto-accept. Overrides are validated and fully audited (original value, override, operator id, timestamp). | `src/Forge.Application/Forecasting/SubmitForecastDecisionHandler.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Forecasting/PredictionOverrideAudit.cs` |
| 14 | **Predictor outage stalls planning.** When the model is unavailable, the system has nothing to plan against. | **Fallback forecast path.** When the predictor is unavailable the Application returns a fallback forecast explicitly flagged as a fallback, so planning continues and the degraded state is visible. | `src/Forge.Application/Forecasting/ForecastingService.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/IMLPredictor.cs` |
| 15 | **Cannot steer a running operation.** Operators cannot change workers-on-shift, speed, open docks, arrival rate, demand, or slotting policy without a restart. | **Operator-adjustable live parameters.** Six live parameters (sim speed, workers on shift, open dock bays, inbound arrival rate, demand multiplier, slotting strategy) are validated and applied to the running system; invalid values are rejected with the offending parameter named and the previous value retained; the updated state is published to clients. | `src/Forge.Application/OperatorParameters/UpdateOperatorParameterHandler.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/OperatorParameters/OperatorParameterService.cs` |
| 16 | **WMS locked to one deployment.** Business logic is entangled with simulation or a specific data feed, so it cannot be reused for a real warehouse. | **Simulation-agnostic WMS_Core behind driver + clock abstractions.** All warehouse rules live in Domain + Application and depend only on `IWarehouseInputDriver`, `IClock`, `IEventBus`, repositories, and `IMLPredictor`. The Simulation driver can be swapped for a real-world driver with no core change. An architecture test enforces that no input-generation logic leaks into the core. | `src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/IWarehouseInputDriver.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/IClock.cs` |
| 17 | **Command paths and read paths tangled together.** Ad-hoc mutation makes behavior hard to test and reason about. | **CQRS-style use-case handlers.** Each use case (create order, record inbound receipt, record temperature, apply per-tick rules, complete task, submit forecast decision, update parameter) is an explicit handler; a read-only snapshot query returns state without mutating it. | `src/Forge.Application/Orders/CreateColonyOrderHandler.cs`, `src/Forge.Application/Queries/GetSimulationSnapshotHandler.cs` |
| 18 | **Lost events / brittle integrations.** Downstream systems miss task and state changes when transport hiccups. | **Event bus behind an abstraction.** Domain events are published through an `IEventBus` seam (in-process in Phase 1); the abstraction lets the transport evolve (RabbitMQ/MassTransit in Phase 2) without changing Application code, and supports retaining unpublished events when degraded. | `src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/IEventBus.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Events/DomainEvents.cs` |
| 19 | **Non-reproducible behavior.** Results differ run-to-run, so bugs cannot be reproduced and audits are impossible. | **Determinism as a first-class property.** FEFO, expiry decay, slotting, labor cost, pathfinding, and reservation contention are all specified and property-tested to be deterministic for identical inputs, making runs reproducible and auditable. | `src/Forge.Domain/Slotting/ZoneIdComparer.cs`, `src/Forge.Domain/Common/Result.cs` |

## Part 2 - Future Work (Deferred Problems and How We Have Prepared for Them)

These are real WMS problems we deliberately deferred beyond Phase 1. In each case the Phase 1 architecture already leaves a seam so the capability can be added later without reworking the layering.

| # | Deferred WMS Problem | Planned Approach | Seam Already In Place |
|---|----------------------|------------------|-----------------------|
| F1 | **Running against a real warehouse (real sensors, scanners, ERP feeds, wall clock).** | Implement a `Real_World_Driver` that feeds the same core inputs from real devices/systems using a wall clock. | The WMS_Core depends only on `IWarehouseInputDriver` and `IClock` (`src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/`); swapping the driver requires no core change. |
| F2 | **Reliable event distribution across processes/services.** | Add a RabbitMQ/MassTransit event bus implementation. | It drops into the existing `IEventBus` abstraction (`src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/IEventBus.cs`) with no Application change. |
| F3 | **Higher forecast accuracy.** | Replace the statistical baseline with trained ML.NET / ONNX demand and risk models. | They implement the existing `IMLPredictor` interface (`src/Forge.Application/Abstractions/IMLPredictor.cs`). |
| F4 | **Inventory accuracy drift (cycle counting).** | Add cycle-count reconciliation and inventory-accuracy tracking. | The `CycleCount` task type is already modeled (`src/Forge.Domain/Tasks/WarehouseTaskType.cs`). |
| F5 | **Low outbound throughput from unbatched picking.** | Add wave/batch planning that groups orders into coordinated pick waves. | Outbound fulfillment already flows through Application use-case handlers and the `WarehouseTask` model (`src/Forge.Domain/Tasks/WarehouseTask.cs`). |
| F6 | **Forward-pick locations running dry.** | Add replenishment flows (reserve -> forward-pick). | Slotting and the zone/capacity model already govern placement (`src/Forge.Application/Slotting/`, `src/Forge.Domain/Capacity/CapacityRule.cs`). |
| F7 | **Labor estimates ignore congestion.** | Add zone-congestion and per-worker productivity curves to labor/travel estimates. | Labor cost, travel time, and worker shifts are already first-class (`src/Forge.Application/Labor/`, `src/Forge.Domain/Labor/`). |
| F8 | **High-performance streaming transport.** | Add gRPC alongside REST/SignalR. | Clients depend only on transport-agnostic Contracts DTOs; the Api and real-time channel sit behind those contracts. |
| F9 | **Richer floor models (multi-floor, dynamic obstacles).** | Extend the grid/path model with more advanced movement. | The `WarehouseGrid`, `Path`, and `PathReservation` abstractions already accommodate more advanced models (`src/Forge.Domain/Spatial/`). |
| F10 | **Interactive mobile client.** | Extend the read-only MAUI monitor into an interactive client and add more monitoring views. | All clients depend only on the Contracts DTOs and the Api layer. |

## Architectural Note - Why the Core Is Reusable

The single most important design decision behind every solution above is the **three-way separation**:

- **WMS_Core** (`Forge.Domain` + `Forge.Application`) - all warehouse business rules, no input-generation logic. This is the reusable, extensible, resilient value component.
- **Input_Driver** (`Forge.Simulation` in Phase 1) - feeds the core the inputs a real warehouse would otherwise supply. Replaceable by a real-world driver.
- **Game** (`Forge.Game`) - pure visualization and operator-control surface; contains no business rules and generates no authoritative demand.

Because the core depends only on abstractions, the same engine that runs this simulation could drive a real warehouse by swapping only the driver - which is exactly what makes Forge a credible WMS/digital-twin foundation rather than just a game.
